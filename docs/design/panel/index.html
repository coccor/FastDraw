The options panel: four ways to organise it

Same selections in every direction: the Orders API box (focal style) and the writes arrow (primary style), with every option the panel offers today. Nothing here is built yet: pick one (or a mix) and I'll change the editor.

Today

Every option is its own section with a heading, all at the same weight, in one long column.

What makes it read as cluttered
  1. 17 sections for a box, 13 for a line, each with the same heading and spacing: nothing says what matters.
  2. Three rows of the same five swatches (background, stroke, text colour) look alike at a glance.
  3. The text of the box, the thing edited most, is at the very bottom, under Icon.
  4. One idea is spread over several rows: stroke is colour + style + width; text is size + font + align + colour.
  5. Rotation, opacity, layers and actions are rare, but sit at full weight between the rest.
A box's panel is 1100px tall; on a laptop it scrolls.

AGrouped inspector

Four groups with small capital headings (Content, Appearance, Text, Arrange) instead of 17 headings. Inside, one line per option: the name on the left, the control on the right. Related controls share a line: a stroke's colour, dash and width are one row; size and font are one row.

  • Every control is still one click away, as today.
  • Content comes first; Arrange is folded shut until opened, and Duplicate / Delete sit in a footer.

BTabs

The panel keeps today's sections but splits them across tabs at the top: Content · Style · Text · Arrange for a box, Line · Label · Arrange for a line. Only one tab shows at a time, so each view is short.

  • Short panel, and each tab can keep today's roomy layout.
  • Options on other tabs are out of sight: changing a fill then a font is two tab switches. The editor remembers the last tab per kind.

CProperty list

One line per property, each saying its current value in words and colour: Stroke ● Red · solid · bold. Clicking a line opens its controls in a popover beside the panel, the way More line ends and the colour picker already do. The panel itself becomes a readable summary of the selection.

  • Shortest panel, and it shows the state of everything at a glance.
  • Every change is two clicks (open, pick). Text is still typed straight into the first line.

DBar over the selection

No sidebar while you draw. A short bar floats just above what is selected, with one button per idea (style, fill, stroke, text, icon or ends), each opening a small popover. Text is typed on the canvas as now. The rare options (rotation, opacity, layers) sit behind ⋯.

  • The whole canvas stays visible, and the controls are where your eyes already are.
  • The bar has to dodge the selection and the edges of the view. With many boxes selected it sits over the group.

My pick: A, the grouped inspector

It removes most of the clutter (four group headings instead of 17, the box panel roughly half as tall, content first, rare options folded away) while every control stays one click away and works exactly as it does now. It is the smallest step from today, so it is also the cheapest to try and to undo.

C reads best and is shortest, but it makes every change two clicks; it could come later for the rarer properties only (Arrange as a property list). B hides options behind tabs, and D is a different editor, worth it only if you want the canvas to be clear of panels.
